The Problem

When the direct path disappears.

Conventional sensing systems depend on a clear, uninterrupted line of sight between transmitter and receiver. In real-world environments, this assumption frequently breaks down.

Existing Approaches

Traditional sensing systems and their limitations.

Each established approach carries fundamental limitations when the sensing environment becomes complex or the direct path is interrupted.

Type 01

Single-Antenna Radar

— Requires direct LOS path to target
— No alternative propagation path
— Severe degradation behind obstacles

Type 02

LiDAR

— Optical line-of-sight strictly required
— Susceptible to weather and obscurants
— Cannot detect beyond solid obstacles

Type 03

Conventional MIMO Radar

— Multipath fading in complex environments
— Limited NLOS coverage without relay nodes
— High signal attenuation through materials
Core Challenges

Four fundamental problems in conventional sensing.

01 Coverage

Limited NLOS Coverage

Conventional systems cannot sense beyond the line of sight. When an obstacle physically blocks the direct propagation path, the receiver receives no usable signal. In urban environments — where buildings, vehicles, and infrastructure constantly interrupt the LOS — detection zones are severely restricted.

02 Attenuation

Signal Attenuation by Obstacles

Even when signals partially penetrate obstacles, they experience significant power loss. At microwave frequencies (8–12 GHz), common building materials and vehicle structures cause substantial attenuation, rapidly reducing the received signal-to-noise ratio below detectable thresholds.

03 Fading

Multipath Fading

In complex environments, signals travel multiple paths and arrive at the receiver with different delays and phases. Destructive interference between these multipath components causes deep fades — momentary severe signal drops that can render a reliable detection impossible even with sufficient signal power.

04 Accuracy

Lower Detection Accuracy in Complex Environments

The cumulative effect of limited coverage, attenuation, and fading is a significant reduction in obstacle detection accuracy. Single-path systems have no redundancy — when the primary path degrades, there is no fallback mechanism to maintain reliable detection performance.

Why It Matters

Why NLOS capability defines modern sensing systems.

"Non-Line-of-Sight is not a rare edge case — it is the dominant condition in real-world deployment environments."

Autonomous vehicles navigate environments saturated with obstacles: other vehicles, buildings, guardrails, and pedestrians. Drones operate in urban canyons and around structures. Industrial robots sense around machinery and equipment.

In every deployment scenario, the ability to detect and locate objects beyond the direct line of sight is not a performance enhancement — it is a fundamental safety requirement. A sensing system that fails in NLOS conditions fails in precisely the situations where reliable detection is most critical.
