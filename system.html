Proposed System

Two paths. One sensing system.

An RIS-aided dual antenna architecture that simultaneously exploits direct and reflected propagation paths, providing resilient sensing in both LOS and NLOS scenarios.

Sensing Workflow

How the system operates.

The sensing workflow exploits both paths simultaneously, combining received signals to make a reliable detection decision regardless of individual path quality.

01

Transmit

The transmitter generates the sensing signal, which is split between the two antennas via a 2-port power splitter.

02

Direct Path Propagation

Antenna 1 sends the signal along the direct LOS path. In unobstructed conditions, this provides the primary detection signal.

03

RIS-Assisted Reflection

Antenna 2 illuminates the RIS panel. The RIS phase-shifts and redirects the signal around obstacles, enabling NLOS coverage.

04

Signal Reception

The receiver collects both the direct and RIS-reflected signals from the environment.

05

Signal Combination

A combiner (e.g., Maximum Ratio Combining) merges both paths to maximize SNR and exploit signal diversity.

06

Obstacle Detection

The signal processor analyzes the combined signal to detect, classify, and locate obstacles in the sensing environment.

Signal Diversity

Why dual antenna?

Antenna 1 — Direct Path

Primary LOS Signal

When a clear line-of-sight exists, Antenna 1 provides the strongest, most direct signal path. Low attenuation and minimal delay make it the primary sensing channel in unobstructed scenarios.

Antenna 2 — RIS-Reflected Path

NLOS Coverage via RIS

The RIS-assisted path creates an intelligent bypass around obstacles. When the direct path is blocked, this path maintains signal reception, preventing total detection failure.

Signal diversity: the core advantage.

By maintaining two independent propagation paths, the system exploits spatial diversity. When one path experiences a deep fade or is physically blocked, the other maintains reception quality.

The key insight is that the two paths rarely fail simultaneously — a direct blockage that eliminates the LOS path typically still allows the RIS-reflected path to function. This independence is the foundation of the system's improved reliability.

Diversity Principle

"Two paths that fail independently provide more reliable communication than one strong path alone."
